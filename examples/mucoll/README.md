# Muon decays into the interaction region — the v0.8 muon collider lattice

Two runs, one decay. A muon of the incoming 5 TeV beam decays in the last few
metres of beam pipe before the interaction point — `mu- -> e- nu_mu_bar nu_e` —
and Geant4 carries a daughter on from the decay point through the real
machine-detector interface: the shrinking bore, the tungsten-alloy nozzle with
its borated liner, the beryllium chamber at the IP.

| Config | What it follows |
|---|---|
| `mudecay_electron_ip.yaml` | the **Michel electron**, ⟨E⟩ = 1.75 TeV, transported through `FTFP_BERT` |
| `mudecay_neutrino_ip.yaml` | the **νμ** of the same decay, same spectrum, with 10¹⁰ cross-section biasing so the ones that reach the nozzle interact |

```bash
gdmltp run     --config examples/mucoll/mudecay_electron_ip.yaml -o e_ip
gdmltp analyze e_ip/output.root -o e_ip/analysis
gdmltp display e_ip/output.root --gdml gdml/mucoll_mdi_v0p8_ip.gdml --all

gdmltp run     --config examples/mucoll/mudecay_neutrino_ip.yaml -o nu_ip
gdmltp compare e_ip/output.root nu_ip/output.root --labels e,numu -o ip_cmp
```

## The geometry

Upstream source: **`dervj/Geant4NuInt`**, branch `add-lattice-gdml`, commit
`110076c` ("Added lattice v0.8 GDML"). Two files are in this repo:

- **`gdml/mucoll_mdi_lattice_v0p8.gdml`** — that export, byte for byte. A
  FLUKA-derived model of the whole machine: tunnel, arcs, rock, the MDI. **It
  cannot be tracked as it stands** (below).
- **`gdml/mucoll_mdi_v0p8_ip.gdml`** — the interaction region cut out of it and
  recentred, which is what the two configs use. Generated by
  `python3 gdml/mucoll_ip_extract.py`; that script is the whole derivation.

### Why the export can't be run as shipped

Three findings, all reproducible from the file itself:

1. **The interaction point is outside the world volume.** `<setup>` names a
   world that is a 10 m cube at the origin, but the MDI it describes is built
   around **(0, −30 m, +85 m)** — that is where the beryllium chamber
   `DET_CHAM_pv` is placed and where the two nozzle tips point. A primary that
   starts outside the world cannot be tracked, so nothing can be fired at this
   IP. Most of the rest of the model (tunnel sections out to z ≈ −250 m, rock
   at y = +142 m) is outside the world too.
2. **The sub-assembly that *is* near the world origin overlaps itself.** On the
   beam axis there, six vacuum cylinders occupy the same space
   (`BEAM_V3U/V3D/V4U/V4D/V5U/V5D`, radii 30/46/62 mm, all rmin = 0, all
   centred on the origin), and at r = 40 mm the tungsten shells `BEAM_SU3/SD3`
   share space with the `BEAM_V4*/V5*` vacuum. Navigation in an overlap is
   undefined, so results from that region would be meaningless.
3. **`CARBON_element` is boron.** It is declared `formula="B" Z="5"` with
   A = 10 — so `BORETH`, the borated polyethylene of the nozzle liner, is
   modelled as hydrogen plus two boron isotopes and contains no carbon at all.
   (Several elements also carry integer A values, FLUKA-style — W as 183, O as
   15 — which is an approximation rather than an error.)

Worth passing back upstream. Nothing here fixes 2 or 3: the extraction keeps
the affected volumes out of the picture rather than editing anyone's physics.

### What the extracted file contains

The MDI assembly is clean — exclusive zones, exactly one volume at every point
tested — so `mucoll_ip_extract.py` keeps those seven volumes, translates them
by (0, +30 m, −85 m) so the **IP is at the origin with the beam along z**, and
sizes the world (16 × 16 × 14 m) to them. Solids and materials are untouched;
the 303 other placements and the solids nothing references any more are
dropped, which takes the file from 835 kB to 113 kB.

| Volume | Material | Extent |
|---|---|---|
| `DET_CHAM` | `G4_Be` | the IP chamber: 1 mm wall, r 23→24 mm, \|z\| ≤ 158 mm |
| `BP_VAC` | vacuum | the bore, \|z\| ≤ 5.95 m (radius below) |
| `LW` / `RW` | `INERM180` (95 % W, 3.5 % Ni, 1.5 % Cu, ρ = 18 g/cm³) | the up/downstream nozzles, \|z\| = 10 mm → 5.95 m, outer radius 1.2 mm at the tip → 554 mm |
| `LBb` / `RBb` | `BORETH` (ρ = 0.918 g/cm³) | the borated liner inside each nozzle, \|z\| = 1.0 → 5.95 m |
| `DET_LAT` | vacuum | the detector cavern the nozzles sit in: r ≤ 6.77 m, \|z\| ≤ 5.95 m |

Radially the nozzle is a sandwich — bore, INERM180, BORETH, INERM180, cavern —
e.g. at z = −3 m: vacuum to r = 9 mm, alloy to 207 mm, borated liner to
287 mm, alloy to 327 mm, then the cavern.

**The bore is the aperture that decides everything here.** It shrinks
linearly from 17.9 mm where it enters this geometry to a 2.85 mm waist at
z = −950 mm, then opens into the beryllium chamber:

| z [mm] | −5900 | −5000 | −4000 | −3000 | −2000 | −1000 | −500 | 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| bore radius [mm] | 17.7 | 15.0 | 12.0 | 9.0 | 6.0 | 3.0 | 4.8 | 23.0 |

## The physics in the beam block

**The decay vertex.** Uniform along the beam axis over the 5.7 m of incoming
pipe this geometry holds (z = −5.9 m to −0.2 m). Uniform is exact, not a
convenience: a 5 TeV muon has γcτ = 47 322 × 658.6 m = **31 168 km**, so it
covers 1.8 × 10⁻⁷ of a decay length crossing this whole region and the decay
rate per metre is flat to seven digits. That number is also the normalisation —
per muon, the probability of decaying inside this geometry at all. With
1.8 × 10¹² muons per bunch (IMCC 10 TeV parameter set), one bunch crossing
puts **≈ 3 × 10⁵ decays** into these 5.7 m, per beam. Each simulated event is
one of those.

**The energy.** Exact angle-integrated lab spectra of the daughters of an
unpolarized in-flight muon decay, in y = E/E_μ with `value` the parent muon
energy:

- `mudecay_e` — the charged lepton: dN/dy = 5/3 − 3y² + 4/3 y³, ⟨E⟩ = 0.35 E_μ
- `mudecay_numu` — the νμ: **the same spectrum** (for an unpolarized muon the
  electron and the νμ share the rest-frame Michel spectrum 2x²(3 − 2x)),
  ⟨E⟩ = 0.35 E_μ
- `mudecay_nue` — the ν̄e: dN/dy = 2 − 6y² + 4y³, ⟨E⟩ = 0.30 E_μ

The three means sum to E_μ, as they must. At E_μ = 5 TeV that is 1.75 TeV for
the electron and the νμ, 1.5 TeV for the ν̄e.

**The direction.** The daughter inherits the parent's direction plus the decay
cone. The cone is 1/γ = **21 μrad** and is negligible next to the beam's own
divergence at the IP, √(ε_N/(γβ\*)) = **0.59 mrad** for ε_N = 25 μm and
β\* = 1.5 mm — so the configs use a 0.6 mrad Gaussian in each transverse slope
and that is what the fan on the display is.

**What that implies.** A daughter born at z₀ clears the 2.85 mm waist only if
its angle is below roughly 2.85 mm / (\|z₀\| − 950 mm): ~0.6 mrad ≈ 1σ for a
decay at the upstream end, many σ for one close in. Sampling the configs'
beam block, **≈ 20 % of the daughters miss the aperture** and run into the
nozzle — the electrons dumping a TeV-scale shower into the alloy, which is the
mechanism behind the beam-induced background, and the neutrinos finding the
only material they will ever cross. The other 80 % fly clean through the IP,
which is equally the point: this is why the decay neutrinos of the last few
metres are a far-field problem and not a detector one.

## Honest caveats

- **No magnetic field.** The export carries none, and in the real machine the
  final-focus optics is what sweeps a 1.75 TeV electron — off-momentum with
  respect to the 5 TeV muon — out of the bore and into the shielding. Here the
  daughter flies ballistically, so the 20 % above is set by the emission angle
  alone and should be read as a lower bound on what the nozzle catches. A
  uniform field can be added with `geant4: {field: ...}`; a real lattice map
  cannot.
- **Transverse beam size is dropped.** Vertices sit exactly on the axis. The
  real envelope is 0.9 µm at the waist growing to ~3 mm at z = −5 m — small
  compared with the bore, and strongly anti-correlated with the divergence
  (the beam converges on the IP), so a fixed σ would be worse than none.
- **Angle and energy are sampled independently.** The true correlation is
  θ ≈ √(E_μ/E − 1)/γ, which puts the soft tail of the spectrum at large angle.
  It matters far less than it sounds: even 1 GeV out of 5 TeV only reaches
  1.5 mrad, and the 0.59 mrad beam divergence dominates the whole distribution.
- **Geant4 is doing the neutrino interaction** in the neutrino config, so the
  front-end prints its "neutrino beam on the bare Geant4 backend" banner. That
  warning is correct: read the energy deposition and the shower, not the
  interaction kinematics. For real interaction physics, point the `genie`
  backend at the same geometry and beam block (`genie: {target: 1000741840}`
  for the nozzle alloy) — and read the spline-generation caveat in
  `examples/maia/README.md` first, because TeV tungsten splines are slow.
- **These runs are not cheap.** A 1.75 TeV electron reaching the alloy makes a
  shower with order 10⁶ steps and g4sim records every step, so `run.events` is
  deliberately small. `geant4: {celeritas: true}` offloads the EM shower.

## Related

decays in the *arcs*, tens of metres upstream, arriving at the MAIA detector as
